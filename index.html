Overview

I study how visual and multimodal media communicate meaning. A particular focus is the production and reception of time-based media such as film and television. My approach is empirical, pairing large corpora of images, video, sound, and text with experimental data. I co-direct the Distant Viewing Lab, where we build new computational methods, open-source software, and open datasets for this work. The research draws on and contributes to media studies, linguistics, cognitive science, and statistics.

Research areas

  • Film and television form

    Media studies · film cognition

    How do moving images communicate? I measure shot length, framing, sound, and editing across large collections of film and television and relate them to genre, industry, and national context, testing claims from film studies and cognitive film theory.

    Selected work: Every Shot, Zero-Shot (2026); The Shortcom Is Not a Short Sitcom (2026); Sitcom Form and Function (2025); Understanding Peanuts and Schulzian Symmetry (2023); Visual Style in Two Network Era Sitcoms (2019).

  • Statistical evidence and methods

    Applied statistics · computational social science

    What can we infer when the corpus is the whole population, and when does a finding about culture replicate? I work on inference and replication for cultural data.

    Selected work: Closed Corpora, Open Questions (2026); A Computational Approach to Statistical Learning (2019); New Data: The Role of Statistics in DH (2019); Generalized Lasso Dual Path Algorithm (2016); Statistical Sleuthing: Olympic Figure Skating (2011).

  • Language, learning, and perception

    Linguistics · cognitive science

    With linguists and psychologists, I study how people produce and perceive language and faces: keystroke logging of second-language writing, automatic scoring of learner speech, cognitive-linguistic approaches to language teaching, and meta-analysis in face perception.

    Selected work: Applied Cognitive Linguistics for Novice Learners (2026); Logging Keystrokes in Writing by English Learners (2024); Whisper for L2 Speech Scoring (2024); P-Curving the Fusiform Face Area (2019); Predicting CEFRL Levels in Learner English (2018).

  • Visual archives and digital humanities

    Digital humanities · information science

    What does it mean to treat images as data? I develop the theory and methods of distant viewing and apply them to historical photographs and other visual archives, building search, annotation, and detection tools suited to how those archives were made.

    Selected work: Matérialité et vision par ordinateur (2026); Explainable Search and Discovery (2024); Distant Viewing (2023); Layered Lives (2022); Uncovering Latent Metadata in the FSA-OWI Photographic Archive (2017).
